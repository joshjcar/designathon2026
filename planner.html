    How these dates are worked out

    1. Your weekly hours are spread evenly across seven days to give a daily budget.
    2. Assessments are handled in deadline order, nearest first.
    3. Each one claims hours backwards from the day before it is due, taking whatever is left unclaimed on each day. The earliest day it claims is its startline.
    4. Because the day budget is shared, a task that arrives late to the queue is pushed further back — which is exactly what happens to you in real life.
    5. Grade due in a week counts each assessment's weight scaled by its course credits, so a 30% assessment in a 4-credit course outranks a 30% assessment in a 3-credit one.

    This is a deliberately simple, explainable rule rather than an optimal schedule. A truly optimal timetable is both computationally hard and impossible to argue with when it tells you something you don't want to hear.
